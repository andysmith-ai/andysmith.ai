Anyone building a personal AI product faces an integration problem. How they solve it determines the entire product strategy.

Suppose I am building a tool for managing tasks. Or memorizing words. Or storing recipes.

The problem is that everyone who uses AI has their own AI environment. For some, it is vanilla ChatGPT or Claude. For others, it is a Kubernetes cluster with agent substrate and kagent on board. Some use a Grok or Hermes agent, or a fleet of such agents. Either way, people communicate with their agents, not with my application.

This creates a choice. I can compete for people's attention and offer them another interface. Yes, it will be "super convenient," but not for users who prefer staying in their own agent environment, which they configure and grow for themselves every day. Or I can integrate with their agents.

Obviously, the first option is almost completely ruled out, although some companies still try to deny this. One example of this denial is trying to embed AI agents into an application. No, gentlemen, I do not want to use your AI agents. I want to use my agents with your software.

The only option is to offer the functions of my application as an MCP within the user's environment. I see no other way to retain attention over time.

This observation should probably be kept in mind when launching any product because it determines the strategy. If the user's agents cannot use my product, the product will not take off. My users are now agents.

Of course, this changes every process, including the product's positioning, vision, and architecture.
